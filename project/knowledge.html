LESSON 01

Understand the main asset classes

Different investments have different claims on assets, cash flows, and returns. The correct comparison depends on your objectives, costs, and risk tolerance.

Simple comparison of common investments
AssetWhat you own Key consideration
Stock A share of ownership in a company Prices and company results can change significantly.
Bond A debt security issued by a borrower Interest-rate and default risks still matter.
Fund / ETF A pooled investment in a basket of assets Check the holdings, fees, and concentration.
Cash / savings Money available for near-term needs Typically lower market volatility, but inflation matters.

LESSON 02

Risk management comes before excitement

Asset allocation is how you divide money among categories such as stocks, bonds, and cash. Diversification means avoiding too much dependence on one company, sector, or asset. Neither eliminates the possibility of loss.

01

Set limits

Decide in advance how much loss or uncertainty your plan can tolerate.

02

Spread exposures

Consider how several holdings may respond to the same event.

03

Understand costs

Small recurring fees and frequent trading costs can affect returns over time.

LESSON 03

Look at the business, not just the ticker

For individual stocks, learn what a company sells, why customers buy it, and whether its financial statements support its story. Revenue describes sales, profit reflects earnings after costs, and cash flow tracks money moving into and out of the business.

Questions to ask: What drives sales? Is debt manageable? How competitive is the industry? What assumptions are already reflected in the stock price?

Fundamental analysis studies a company's business and finances. Technical analysis studies price and volume behavior. Neither removes market uncertainty or replaces careful risk management.
